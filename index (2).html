Teaching & Design

Student time is valuable. I don't waste it.

In education, we often overload the learner hoping something will stick. I've found the opposite is usually true. Less is more.

Every assignment earns its place.

I don't add content because a course has always had it. I ask what a student is supposed to walk away knowing or being able to do. If an assignment can't answer that, it goes.

Competency has to drive the design.

Some students arrive already able to do the thing. They should get to prove it and move on. Others need explanation, practice, and time. Good CBE is built for both.

In practice

When the state fair goes sideways

For a public safety course, I put students in the middle of a state fair crisis: a spreading illness, rumors online, a "fair takeover." They had to act as the public information officer, not define crisis communication in the abstract.

In practice

What if the whole course fit on a phone?

For an English course, I asked what we were actually trying to teach once I stripped away the assumption that "real" academic writing required Microsoft Word. I built a course students could complete on their phone.

What I don't do

  • I don't turn a classroom assignment into an online one and call it redesigned.
  • I don't build discussion boards where the goal is a reply that starts with "Great post, Janet."
  • I don't keep an assignment or activity just because it's familiar. It has to serve the learner.
